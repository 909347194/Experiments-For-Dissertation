Differential evolution (DE) is widely adopted for multi-UAV cooperative path
planning, yet its search behaviour is governed by hyper-parameters that are
usually tied together by hand-designed equations. In the discrete-mapping DE
(DMDE) formulation the crossover rate \CR{} determines the scale factor
\Ffactor{} and the extinction rate \GMR{} through fixed formulas, so the three
mechanisms can only move together. This paper proposes an LLM-enhanced DE
framework that decouples these three mechanisms and delegates their
coordination to a large language model (LLM) without changing the underlying
evolutionary operators. At fixed intervals the LLM observes constraint
feasibility, population diversity, fitness improvement, the outcome of its own
previous decision and a fixed-\CR{} shadow population, and then adjusts \CR{},
\Ffactor{} and \GMR{} independently. We evaluate the framework on two scenarios
-- a balanced assignment task and a scarce-resource task -- against a vanilla
DE baseline, and we additionally isolate the effect of the action space by
comparing a decoupled, a coupled and a CR-locked control condition.
